[Olympics, 1968]

Description

This poster commemorates one of the most widely reproduced images in Olympic history: sprinters Tommie Smith and John Carlos raising black-gloved fists during the medal ceremony for the 200-meter race at the 1968 Mexico City Games. Produced around 1969 by Personality Posters as part of what became known as the Yanker poster collection, it turned a single photograph of protest into a mass-produced object of its own — meant to circulate the image, and the statement it made about racial inequality in the United States and in sport, well beyond the stadium where it happened.

As an object, it sits right at the intersection of sports analytics' usual subject matter — athletic achievement, records, medal counts — and the harder-to-quantify history of how race has shaped who gets to compete, how athletes are treated, and what platforms they're allowed to use once they've won.

Why This Object

Smith and Carlos were members of the Olympic Project for Human Rights, which had pushed for a boycott of the 1968 Games over racial discrimination in and beyond sport. Their gesture led to their immediate suspension from the U.S. team. A poster like this one is a reminder that the "digitized cultural object" isn't only the original photograph — it's also the later objects, like this print, that communities produced to keep circulating and reinterpreting an event.
